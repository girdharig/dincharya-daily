Traditions we cover

Choose one or several. Your daily message only includes the traditions you pick.

Hindu

Each weekday has a deity and a simple observance, such as a lamp for Lakshmi on Friday or a light fast on Monday for Shiva. Festival days like Diwali, Chhath Puja, Gita Jayanti and Makar Sankranti arrive ahead of time, with the fasts and rituals that go with them.

Christian

Sundays and the church year: All Saints', Advent, Christmas, Epiphany and Orthodox Christmas. Weekdays bring a short Scripture reading, an evening examen or a prayer, and Friday abstinence for those who keep it.

Muslim

The weekly rhythm of Jumu‘ah on Friday and the voluntary Monday and Thursday fasts, the White Days each month, and the nights of Isra and Mi‘raj and Mid-Sha‘ban. Daily remembrance, Quran reading and charity, with stories from the life of the Prophet and his companions.

Buddhist

Daily meditation and mindfulness practices, from metta to walking meditation, and Uposatha days at the new and full moon. Festivals such as Loy Krathong and Bodhi Day, and stories such as Kisa Gotami and the mustard seed.

Jewish

Shabbat each week, with an Erev Shabbat reminder on Friday. Rosh Chodesh, Hanukkah, Asara B'Tevet and Tu BiShvat, plus Torah reading days, tzedakah and daily study. Stories from the sages such as Hillel and Honi.

Sikh

Nitnem and naam simran through the week, sewa and kirat karni, Sangrand and Gurpurab on the day. Shaheedi days, Parkash Purab and Maghi, and stories from the life of Guru Nanak.

A note on dates

Many festivals follow lunar calendars, and communities or regions can observe them on different days. We show the most widely observed date and mention when it varies. If your community follows another, trust your community.
