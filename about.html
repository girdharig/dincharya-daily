About Dincharya

Dincharya means "daily routine" in Sanskrit. We help people keep the rhythm of their own tradition without having to remember every date.

HinduChristianMuslimBuddhistJewishSikh

Why we built it

Festival dates move with the moon. Fasts fall on different days. Practices are easy to forget when life is busy. Calendars show dates, but they rarely say what the day is for or what to do. Dincharya puts the day, a small practice and a short story into one calm message.

What we believe

Respect first

Every tradition is described in its own terms. We don't rank traditions or blend their practices together.

Accuracy over volume

An entry only appears after it is marked as reviewed. Observance differs between communities and regions, so we describe common practice and say so when it varies. If you spot a mistake, please tell us.

Your attention is yours

One message a day at the time you choose. No ads. You can change your traditions or stop in one tap.

Minimal data

We keep only what we need to send your message: email, your traditions, time zone and send time, plus your WhatsApp number if you add it. See the privacy page.

Where we are now

Dincharya currently covers Hindu, Christian, Muslim, Buddhist, Jewish and Sikh traditions, in English. We are adding more festivals, traditions and languages, and your feedback decides what comes next. Share an idea, or tell us about your tradition or region.
